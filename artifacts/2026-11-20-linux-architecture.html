The three engines

Claude, Codex and Gemini are each installed once and shared. A home isn't a copy of an engine; it's a folder the engine is pointed at, plus a small server that talks to it. Adding a home costs a folder, not another install.

The engines' raw logs expire after about 30 days. The notes and compendiums carry what matters.

Five rules that keep it this way

  • One place per thing. A file lives in one spot; homes point to the Library instead of copying (today about 7 GB is duplicates).
  • Installing means listing. No line in the inventory, no install.
  • Finishing means clearing. A piece isn't done until its bench is empty.
  • MP3 for auditions, WAV only for the final master (Antigravity's idea; Suno WAVs are most of the audio).
  • Back up the small set: masters, notes and identity files, outside Linux, now and then.
