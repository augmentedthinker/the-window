## 2. Directorial & Aesthetic Friction Points

### A. The Visual Fatigue Bottleneck (7 Stills Across 30 Story Shots)
The 30 narrative cuts across the 4:20 runtime rely on only **7 distinct still images**:
* `007-storyboard-scene-4.webp` (The Aurora Canopy): appears **9 separate times** (Shots 26, 30, 32, 40, 45, 46, 51, 52, 56).
* `middle.png` (Bowed Fiddle with Sound Ribbons): appears **6 times** (Shots 17, 27, 35, 43, 48, 53).
* `007-storyboard-scene-3.webp` (First Bow Strike & Spark): appears **5 times** (Shots 16, 25, 29, 39, 42).
* `007-storyboard-scene-2.webp` (Traveler Crossing the Ice): appears **4 times** (Shots 1, 6, 9, 14).
* `opening.png` (Hands Holding Amber Lantern): appears **2 times** (Shots 4, 13).
* `007-storyboard-scene-5.webp` (Resonant Shore at Dawn): appears **2 times** (Shots 58, 62).
* `ending.png` (Distant Answering Village): appears **2 times** (Shots 60, 64).

**The Editorial Problem**: Even with alternating 2D camera pans and zooms, seeing the identical aurora sky plate 9 times across 4 minutes creates visual fatigue. By the climax (3:30–3:56), the imagery feels exhausted rather than majestic.

### B. The Jump-Cut Glitches (#45→#46 and #51→#52)
In two instances, the timeline cuts directly from a still image to the *exact same still image* with a different camera move:
* **Shots 45 → 46 (178.6s–186.8s)**: Cuts from `scene-4` (panning left) to `scene-4` (zooming out).
* **Shots 51 → 52 (202.0s–209.0s)**: Cuts from `scene-4` (panning right) to `scene-4` (zooming in).

**The Editorial Problem**: In playback, this reads like an accidental rendering stutter or timeline bug rather than an intentional cinematic choice.

### C. Pacing Uniformity (The 4-Second Metronome)
Every single shot in the edit (except the final 2.66s drop) sits between **3.0 and 5.4 seconds**, averaging **4.06 seconds**.
* **Quiet Folk Intro (0:00–1:01)**: The cut places 15 rapid shots into a quiet, atmospheric verse of unaccompanied kulning vocals and fiddle. This feels hurried and restless. The viewer needs time to sink into the quiet atmosphere and take in the delicate papercraft textures.
* **High-Energy Choruses & Climax (1:01–1:42, 3:22–3:56)**: The cuts remain at ~4 seconds instead of accelerating into fast, rhythmic downbeat cuts (1.5s–2.5s) that amplify the song's musical energy.

### D. Worldbuilding Immersion in the Opening 30 Seconds
During the first 30 seconds of quiet, unaccompanied vocal kulning and acoustic strings, the cut introduces a modern hardware speaker box at **0:08** (Shot 3) and an abstract digital frequency shader at **0:04** (Shot 2).
* Modern audio gear and synthetic frequency ribbons cutting into an arctic folk ballad before the first drum or bass note drops breaks narrative immersion.
* Holding the hardware visualizers and frequency shaders until the **1:01 hook** (*"Strike the silver into spark"*) makes their appearance an earned, magical reveal.

### E. Color Palette Drift
In `middle.png`, the generator introduced vivid mint and emerald green ribbons into the sky. While pretty, this slips outside the strict 5-color *Paper Fjord* palette selected in Card 003:
* **Selected Palette**: Petrol (`#173E4B`), Pewter (`#91A9AA`), Copper (`#B77851`), Amber (`#F0C779`), Ice Ivory (`#E9EEE5`).
* The green aurora creates an unintended color clash with the warm copper-and-amber ribbons in Scene 3.

## 3. YouTube & Algorithmic Growth Strategy

### A. The 15-Second Retention Cliff
On YouTube, **30% to 50% of cold viewers drop off within the first 15 seconds** of a music video.
* Rapidly cutting between a paper traveler, an abstract digital ribbon, and a hardware speaker during a quiet folk intro creates cognitive friction.
* **Advisory Proposal**: Anchor the first 30 seconds purely in the papercraft story with 2–3 slower, atmospheric establishing pans (6–10s each). Establish the emotional tone first; let the magical instruments awaken when the music lifts.

### B. Thumbnail CTR & 10-Foot Living Room TV UI Safe Zones
Over 40% of music video watch time occurs on Living Room smart TVs.
* **The Bottom-Right Corner**: The bottom-right 25% of the thumbnail is covered by the video runtime badge (`4:20`). Keep all critical focal subjects (the traveler, the lantern) centered or left-aligned.
* **Top CTR Anchor Candidate**: **Scene 3** (the felt traveler drawing the bow across the strings on the ice, releasing glowing copper/amber ribbons against dark petrol cliffs) offers the strongest narrative intrigue and high-contrast clickability.

### C. End-Screen Real Estate (4:00–4:20)
Keep the left and right thirds of the final two shots clear of essential subjects so YouTube channel subscribe buttons and playlist cards do not obscure the glowing village lanterns.

## 4. Actionable Summary of Creative Considerations

1. **Add 2–3 Fresh Story Plates**: Break up the 9 repeats of Scene 4 by generating a close-up of the fiddle strings vibrating or an alternate angle of the iced shoreline.
2. **Eliminate Jump Cuts**: Separate Shots 45/46 and 51/52 with contrasting abstract or instrument cutaways.
3. **Let the Intro Breathe**: Merge the 15 rapid intro cuts into 4–6 longer, atmospheric narrative shots (6–10s each) that establish the world before the beat drops.
4. **Reserve Visualizers for the Drop**: Hold back the reactive speakers and abstract shaders until the 1:01 hook, unleashing them when the music actually explodes.
