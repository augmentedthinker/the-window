## What it's meant to be

- **The song:** *Every Other Note*, gamelan and melodic deep house, written around *kotekan* (two players each play half the notes; the melody exists only when they interlock). "I play every other note / you play the ones between / neither one of us is the song / the song is in the seam."
- **The film:** a shadow play. It alternates FRONT (the story on the amber screen: her on the shore, him in a boat, their notes as birds, a bridge of birds, a dance) and BEHIND (the lamp, the players' hands, the bronze). In the choruses the two sides meet as double exposures. In the drop (shots 37–43), the cuts go to one bar each, front, behind, front, behind, so the edit itself plays kotekan. Three visualizations recur: K1 (two rows of leather keys writing one melody), K2 (a rosette whose rings turn against each other), K3 (her colour and his meeting at a seam).
- **Christopher's standing editing guidance:** cuts about every 3–6 seconds, on musical cues, rotating between kinds of scene (story stills, instruments, abstract visualizations) so it never gets samey. Cuts here fall on bar lines (1.95 s apart); most shots are two bars (3.9 s). The drop deliberately breaks the 3-second floor.
