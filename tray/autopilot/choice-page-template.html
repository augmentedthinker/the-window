A · Brass & motion

Midnight on the Moving Floor

Sound. A swaggering, upbeat brass-and-bass dance track: a punchy horn riff, elastic electric bass, crisp live drums and a soulful lead vocal. The chorus opens into a big, joyous call-and-response.

Feeling. A night that begins with someone standing on the edge of the room and ends with them letting themselves join in. Playful, a little nervous, then gloriously free.

Visual world. A late-night dance hall assembled from rich cut-paper shapes: amber lamps, deep plum shadows and flashes of vermilion. A dancer crosses the floor as brass-shaped light and a floor-standing speaker answer the rhythm.

B · Folk & horizon

The Long Way to Daylight

Sound. Intimate acoustic folk that grows into a wide, driving anthem: fingerpicked guitar, weathered lead voice, brushed percussion, cello and a rising group harmony. The pulse gathers like footsteps, without losing the human closeness.

Feeling. Two travelers carrying different kinds of grief discover they don’t have to mend each other to walk together. The hope is earned, quiet at first, then expansive.

Visual world. A windswept coastal path in painted textures, slate blue and sea-glass green. The travelers pass a small radio tower and leave a line of light along the trail; waves and a handmade receiver could become visual instruments as the song grows.

C · Electronic & wonder

Garden of Machines

Sound. A curious, rhythm-led electronic track with marimba-like synths, syncopated clicks, deep rounded sub-bass and an airy, wordless vocal hook. It starts sparse and builds into a bright, intricate groove.

Feeling. Wonder and mischief: a little machine discovers an abandoned greenhouse and accidentally wakes its sleeping garden. Each new sound feels like another thing coming to life.

Visual world. A lush glasshouse of copper, moss and electric turquoise. Mechanical flowers unfurl, a tiny robot threads between them, and glowing meters and abstract shapes have room to perform with the music.
